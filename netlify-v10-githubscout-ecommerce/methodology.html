How the scan actually works

No black box. This page lists every source we check, how confidence is scored, and how savings estimates are grounded — including what we refuse to estimate.

Confidence scoring

Confidence is a corroboration score, not a probability. It is set by the kind of evidence and how independent the sources are — never by how many substrings happened to match. Every detection lands in one of three bands:

BandWhat it takesScore
DetectedTwo or more independent sources agree, or a third-party script host was parsed off a page we actually fetched — direct evidence the app's code loads on the storefront.70–95
LikelyExactly one storefront page matched a pattern and nothing else corroborates it.60
PossibleThe only evidence is a DNS record, a robots.txt reference, or a lone substring. That proves a domain relationship, not that the app runs on the store.25

Possible-strength signals never produce dollars. They are listed in the report so you can see what we saw, but they are excluded from the savings band, the overlap costing, and every other dollar figure. Confidence caps at 95% — external scanning can never be 100% certain, and we won't pretend otherwise.

Cost benchmarks — what they are and aren't

Every dollar figure in a report is a benchmark: typical published mid-tier pricing from the vendor's own pricing page (cited in the full report). It is not your invoice. Your actual bill may be higher or lower. Free and native tools are marked $0 and never counted toward savings.

Savings estimates — grounded or absent

The savings band is 15–40% of the benchmarked cost of paid apps we actually detected — the typical consolidation/downgrade outcome when a stack has overlap. Four rules we enforce in code:

1. No crawl, no estimate. A savings figure is produced only if the scan confirmed the URL is a Shopify storefront and fetched at least one page from it. Fail either and the report explains why the number is withheld — it never shows a smaller one instead.
2. No detections, no estimate. If the scan finds no paid apps on the storefront, the report says "no estimate" instead of inventing a number.
3. Your ad-spend answer never changes the dollars. It only sets urgency framing.
4. Overlaps are itemized. Two paid review tools? The report names both, shows both benchmarks, and prices the consolidation explicitly.

Limits we're upfront about

Server-side integrations, consent-gated scripts, and tag-manager-injected tools can be invisible to external scanning. When the scan sees little, the report says so and recommends an admin-side audit rather than guessing. The scanner fetches only public pages, respects a strict size/time budget, and never authenticates to your store.

Some stores refuse automated traffic outright. When bot protection returns a refusal or a challenge page instead of the store, the report says the store blocked the scan — it does not report that as an empty stack. Nothing is estimated from a scan that saw nothing.

How the scanner requests pages

The scanner sends the same request headers a desktop browser sends, and fetches a fixed, small set of pages any logged-out visitor can reach: your homepage, your cart page, one product page, /robots.txt, and /products.json. It reads each response once, caps response size and time, and never logs in, submits a form, or places an order. Contact details for the crawler are published at /.well-known/security.txt.
